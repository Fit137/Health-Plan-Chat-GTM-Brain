# Value Proposition Framework
**Health Plan Chat · Sprint 1 · August 2026**

Structured to the T2D3 Value Proposition Framework Template: brand promise and positioning,
three value proposition pillars with three reasons to believe each, then a copy drill for
every reason to believe (nine tables). Copy follows the template's own rule — written to and
about the reader, in terms of what they gain, not what the software does.

## Value proposition 1: Plan Fluency

### Table 2 · Reason to believe (1.1)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Reason to believe (1)** | Trained on the actual Summary of Benefits and Evidence of Coverage documents for the plans you sell. |
| **Applicable Persona(s)**<br>*This section is getting at the who.* | Agency Owner / Principal Broker · Licensed Medicare Agent · Front Desk / Office Administrator |
| **Headline**<br>*Main and short copy.* | It already knows your plans. |
| **Tier 2 Copy (25 words)** | Your agent answers from the actual Summary of Benefits and Evidence of Coverage for every plan you sell — not generic Medicare information scraped off the internet. |
| **Tier 3 Copy (50+ words)** | A single Summary of Benefits runs past fifty pages, and you carry a shelf of them. Right now that knowledge lives in your head and in the heads of the agents you trained, which is why every hard question routes back to you. Hand over the documents for the plans you sell and your front line reads them the way your best agent would — the same numbers, the same plan year, the same county. The caller who reaches you at nine on a Sunday gets the answer, not a promise that someone will call them back. |

## Value proposition 2: Answers You Can Stand Behind

### Table 5 · Reason to believe (2.1)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Reason to believe (1)** | Every answer resolves against the carrier's own document, so any claim can be traced back to its source. |
| **Applicable Persona(s)** | Agency Owner / Principal Broker · Compliance Officer · Director of Operations |
| **Headline** | Every answer has a source. |
| **Tier 2 Copy (25 words)** | Nothing is improvised. Each answer resolves against the carrier's own plan document, so you can show exactly where it came from when someone asks. |
| **Tier 3 Copy (50+ words)** | The risk with a general-purpose bot is not that it sounds wrong — it is that it sounds right while inventing a copay. You carry that liability, not the vendor. Your agent works the other way round: it answers only what the carrier's own document supports, and every response can be traced to the page it came from. When a client questions what they were told, or a carrier asks, you have the document and the recording rather than a recollection. |

### Table 6 · Reason to believe (2.2)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Reason to believe (2)** | Bounded by design — plan-specific guidance escalates to your licensed agent with the whole conversation carried across. |
| **Applicable Persona(s)** | Licensed Medicare Agent · Agency Owner / Principal Broker · Compliance Officer |
| **Headline** | It knows where to stop. |
| **Tier 2 Copy (25 words)** | Advice belongs to your licensed agent. Your AI answers the factual questions, then hands over warm — with the full conversation already attached. |
| **Tier 3 Copy (50+ words)** | An agent who never escalates is a liability; one who escalates everything is a phone tree. Yours draws the line where the rules do — factual questions about a plan get answered, anything that becomes advice goes to a licensed human. And the handoff carries the conversation with it, so your agent picks up knowing who is calling, which plan they asked about and what has already been covered. Nobody repeats themselves, and the licensed conversation stays licensed. |

### Table 7 · Reason to believe (2.3)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Reason to believe (3)** | TPMO disclaimer, automated-system disclosure and call recording handled automatically, retained to the CMS ten-year standard. |
| **Applicable Persona(s)** | Compliance Officer · Agency Owner / Principal Broker · Director of Operations |
| **Headline** | Compliance that happens by itself. |
| **Tier 2 Copy (25 words)** | The disclaimer gets read, the caller is told they are speaking to an automated system, and the recording is kept. Every call, without anyone remembering. |
| **Tier 3 Copy (50+ words)** | CMS expects the TPMO disclaimer before benefits are discussed, an automated system to say what it is, and every sales call recorded and kept for ten years. Today that depends on people remembering under pressure in the busiest six weeks of your year. Built into the call, it simply happens — on the first call and the four hundredth. An audit or a complaint stops being a scramble through voicemail and becomes a search. |

## Value proposition 3: Coverage Without the Headcount

### Table 8 · Reason to believe (3.1)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Reason to believe (1)** | Answers every call, night and weekend, in your agency's name — no phone tree, no queue, no AEP overflow going to voicemail. |
| **Applicable Persona(s)** | Agency Owner / Principal Broker · Sales Manager · Front Desk / Office Administrator |
| **Headline** | Your office never closes. |
| **Tier 2 Copy (25 words)** | Every call answered in your agency's name, at nine on a Sunday and at peak AEP, however many come in at once. |
| **Tier 3 Copy (50+ words)** | A Medicare lead goes cold in about five minutes, and the calls you most want do not arrive between nine and five. Between the fifteenth of October and the seventh of December they arrive all at once. Your front line picks up every one of them, in your agency's name, however many are ringing — so the caller who would have hit voicemail and dialled the next agency stays with you instead. A single member who enrols and stays five years is worth roughly two thousand dollars. |
