## 2026-08-24 · Lead on plan grounding, not on the vectors competitors lead — **DECIDED**

**Verdict.** Position on plan-grounded answer depth (9.3) and accessible entry for a small
agency (8.7). Do not build the strategy on deployment speed or integration breadth.

**Rationale.** The brief invited a strategy built on vectors 1 and 2, which are the
vectors Synthflow leads and where we score 5.4 and 4.6. Three problems: it is an execution
race on someone else's ground, it contradicts the feature analysis (all six Q1
differentiators are L3 plan IP, zero of fourteen L1 features reach Q1), and leading a
category from last place on your chosen vectors is not a plan.

**Did not change.** Onboarding speed remains urgent — reframed from differentiator to
qualifying threshold, which raises its priority. Integrations still matter; breadth does
not. Full reasoning: `reference/positioning-verdict.md`.

## 2026-08-24 · The wedge is a pricing wedge before it is a product wedge — **DECIDED**

**Verdict.** Lead with accessible, predictable pricing. Treat plan depth as the proof that
the price is not a downgrade, not the other way round.

**Rationale.** OF-1. careCycle is not losing the independent segment on capability; they
are not competing for it. The competitive risk is not careCycle winning our deals, it is
careCycle launching a self-serve tier.

**Did not change.** Plan grounding is still what we lead the *message* with. This decision
is about the order of the argument, not its content.

## Open, owned elsewhere

Questions deliberately not answered in this brain. Each needs someone outside it to settle
it. Recorded rather than invented, because an invented answer becomes a rule and a wrong
rule propagates into every asset.

| — | Can onboarding get under five business days reliably? | Eng | First design partner |

**Q-1, Q-2 and the careCycle price are all settleable with three phone calls.** They are
the cheapest open items in the brain and two of them touch the core positioning claim.

**GAP-1 was found by the build's own live test** and is the one to close first. The
positioning says acquisition happens by demonstration rather than persuasion — but the
brain holds no specimen of the thing being demonstrated. There is no verbatim example of a
plan-grounded answer: what it says, how long it is, how it cites the carrier document,
what it sounds like when it declines and escalates.

Every high-value asset needs it. The landing page demonstration section, the sandbox
handover, the "what it will and won't say" one-pager, and the sales conversation all
resolve to the same missing artifact. One captured transcript closes all four.

Until it exists, any agent asked for a landing page will either omit the demonstration —
gutting the page — or invent a specimen answer, which would be a fabricated product claim.
Neither is acceptable, so the honest instruction is: **ask for the transcript rather than
writing around it.**
