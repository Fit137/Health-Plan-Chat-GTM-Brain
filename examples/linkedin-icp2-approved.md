# Approved example — LinkedIn touch 1, ICP-2

**The voice reference for the ICP-2 track.** Note how little it has in common with the
ICP-1 example, which is the point.

---

**To:** Director of Operations, GA with ~180 contracted downline agents

> Ran answer-quality audits across 40 Medicare agencies this quarter. The finding I did
> not expect: variance inside a single downline was wider than variance between
> independent agencies.
>
> Same plan, same county, four contracted agents, four different answers on the dental
> maximum. Two of the four were wrong.
>
> For a downline your size that is a training problem you cannot solve by training,
> because the people turning over are the people you trained.
>
> Would it be useful to see that run across one of your segments?

---

## Why this works

**It leads with data the reader does not have about their own business.** A Director of
Operations cannot audit 180 contracted agents by hand. This is management information,
offered before any ask.

**The finding is counter-intuitive and specific.** "Variance inside a downline was wider
than between agencies" is a real finding with a real number behind it. It earns attention
because it is not the thing they expected.

**It names the failure mode in their language.** "A training problem you cannot solve by
training" is the sentence that makes this buyer lean in, because it describes something
they have already experienced and not articulated.

**It sells enforceability, not plan knowledge.** The ICP-1 pitch — "it knows your plans" —
lands as a nice-to-have here. Uniform control across people you do not employ is the
thing this buyer actually cannot buy anywhere else.

**No product, no price, no security claim.** All three come later, and the security posture
comes from us before they go looking.

**The ask is a segment audit, not a meeting.** Same principle as ICP-1: the smallest
possible yes.

---

## What would break it

- Mentioning month-to-month pricing. That is the ICP-1 offer and it invites a procurement
  question about why the terms differ.
- Any SOC 2 implication. We do not hold it and this reader verifies.
- Claiming multi-agency deployment is unique (OF-2). Coverage Voice ships it.
- Sending this to an independent agency owner. Wrong pitch, prospect lost. Title-verify
  first.
