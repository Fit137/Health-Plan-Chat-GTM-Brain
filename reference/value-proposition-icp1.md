## Brand promise

Every caller gets a straight answer about their Medicare plan — the dental allowance, the
copay, the OTC card — at the moment they ask, in your agency's name, whether or not anyone
is in the office.

## Positioning

The only AI front line trained on the specific Medicare plans your agency sells, answering
beneficiaries directly from the carrier's own plan documents — at a price an independent
agency can actually carry.

**The price clause is load-bearing.** Drop it and the uniqueness claim is exposed to Q-1
(whether careCycle covers pre-sale benefit Q&A). Keep the two halves together.

## Pillar 1 · Plan Fluency

Your callers ask about their plan, not about Medicare in general. They want to know what
their dental maximum is, whether their podiatrist is covered, what the OTC card actually
pays for. Your AI answers those questions from the plan documents you sell, so the person
who calls at nine on a Sunday gets the same answer your best agent would give on a Tuesday
morning.

**1.1 — Trained on the actual Summary of Benefits and Evidence of Coverage documents for
the plans you sell.**
Headline: *It already knows your plans.*
25 words: Your agent answers from the actual Summary of Benefits and Evidence of Coverage
for every plan you sell — not generic Medicare information scraped off the internet.

**1.2 — Answers the supplemental benefit questions callers actually ask: dental, vision,
hearing, transportation, OTC and flex allowances, with your plan's real numbers.**
Headline: *The questions they actually call about.*
25 words: Dental maximums, hearing aid allowances, the OTC card balance, rides to
appointments. The questions that fill your phone, answered with your plan's real numbers.

**1.3 — Handles premium, copay and cost-sharing questions for a named plan, and compares
across the plans in your book.**
Headline: *What it costs, plan by plan.*
25 words: Premiums, copays and cost-sharing for a specific plan — and an honest comparison
across the plans you carry, so callers arrive already informed.

## Pillar 2 · Answers You Can Stand Behind

The reason most people distrust an AI on a coverage question is that most AI deserves it.
Yours does not guess. Every answer traces to a carrier document, anything plan-specific
hands to your licensed agent with the full conversation attached, and the disclaimer and
recording happen without anyone remembering to do them.

**2.1 — Every answer resolves against the carrier's own document, so any claim can be
traced back to its source.**
Headline: *Every answer has a source.*
25 words: Nothing is improvised. Each answer resolves against the carrier's own plan
document, so you can show exactly where it came from when someone asks.

**2.2 — Bounded by design: plan-specific guidance escalates to your licensed agent with
the whole conversation carried across.**
Headline: *It does not guess. It escalates.*

**2.3 — TPMO disclaimer, automated-system disclosure and call recording handled
automatically, retained to the CMS ten-year standard.**
Headline: *The compliance steps happen whether or not anyone remembers them.*

## Pillar 3 · Coverage Without the Headcount

You cannot hire your way to an eleven-o'clock Saturday, and you should not have to spend
enterprise money to cover one.

**3.1 — Answers every call, night and weekend, in your agency's name. No phone tree, no
queue, no AEP overflow going to voicemail.**
Headline: *You cannot hire your way to an eleven-o'clock Saturday.*

**3.2 — Published, predictable pricing sized for a three-to-ten agent agency, month to
month.**
Headline: *A price you can read without booking a call.*
**Status: this RTB is not yet usable externally.** Pricing is unpublished (OF-1). The
claim becomes true the day the price page ships, and not before.

**3.3 — Answers in Spanish from the same plan documents, so a bilingual market converts
without a bilingual hire.**
Headline: *Bilingual without the hire.*

## Which pillar to lead with

| Situation | Lead with |
|---|---|
| Cold, no prior contact | Pillar 1. The gap explains itself |
| They have raised accuracy or liability | Pillar 2, and do not soften it |
| They have raised cost or headcount | Pillar 3, minus 3.2 until price is published |
| They are comparing against a $79 receptionist | Pillar 1 only. Never argue on price against a commodity |
| They are comparing against careCycle | Pillar 3's economics, with Pillar 1 as the proof it is not a downgrade |

## The four claims to check before publishing

Three of the nine RTBs rest on capabilities not confirmed against the product (Q-3), and
one on unpublished pricing (OF-1). Before any of this goes live:

1. Confirm provider lookup, consent capture and accessibility design — or cut every
   sentence that implies them. They do not appear above, deliberately.
2. Publish the price, or cut 3.2.
