# Objection handling

*Synthesized from the ICP blockers, the competitive gaps and the claim ledger. Load before
any sales conversation. Where the honest answer is "not yet", it says not yet — an
objection file that treats every objection as a misunderstanding trains people to argue
with prospects.*

---

### "It'll say something wrong and I'll get blamed."

The top ICP-1 objection, and it deserves its own asset rather than a line.

It does not improvise. Every answer resolves against the carrier's own plan document, and
we can show you which one. Anything that needs licensed judgement — plan recommendations,
eligibility edge cases, anything outside the documents you gave us — stops and hands to
your agent with the whole conversation attached. Before handover we run twenty of your
hardest plan questions against your own documents and show you the results. If it is not
twenty out of twenty, it does not go live.

### "Can an AI really know *my* plans, or just Medicare in general?"

That is the right question and it is the whole difference. Generic assistants know what
Medicare Advantage is. Yours reads the Summary of Benefits and Evidence of Coverage for
the plans you actually sell, in your county, for this plan year. Give us your hardest plan
question and we will answer it in the sandbox on your own documents. That takes 72 hours
and costs nothing.

### "How is this different from careCycle?"

careCycle is a serious product with real Medicare depth. The difference is who they sell
to: roughly ten thousand dollars a month for an operation your size, behind a demo and a
custom quote. They are not losing agencies like yours — they are not selling to them. We
are the same depth at a price an independent agency can carry.

*If the prospect is genuinely enterprise-scale, say careCycle may be the right answer for
them today. It costs one deal and buys the credibility that wins the next five.*

### "Coverage Voice can be live in 48 hours. How long do you take?"

Five business days from the day we receive your plan documents, and most of that is
reading them properly. They are faster because they are not ingesting a plan corpus. What
you get for the extra three days is an agent that can answer the dental allowance question
instead of taking a message about it.

*Do not oversell this. Deployment speed is our weakest vector and a prospect who has
already spoken to Coverage Voice knows it.*

### "Are you SOC 2 certified?"

No. It is on the roadmap and we are not going to imply otherwise. Coverage Voice is
certified and if that is a hard requirement in your procurement, they clear a bar we do
not yet. Here is our actual posture: [encryption at rest and in transit, access controls,
CMS ten-year retention, BAA available]. If you want, our compliance review will document
exactly where we sit against your requirements, including where we fall short.

### "Does it book the appointment?"

Not yet — it is on the pre-AEP roadmap. Today it hands the qualified caller to your
licensed agent with the full conversation attached, and drops a structured lead into your
CRM. If booking is a requirement rather than a preference, say so and I will tell you
honestly whether to wait.

### "What about Scope of Appointment?"

Not yet, and I want to be straight that Coverage Voice captures it in-call at exactly the
moment we hand off. It is on the roadmap alongside appointment booking. Today the SOA
happens where it happens now — with your licensed agent.

### "What does it cost?"

*Until the price is published, there is no compliant answer to this question. Do not
improvise one. Say:* Pricing is being finalised and I am not going to quote you a number I
might have to change. What I can tell you is the shape: a flat monthly fee per location
with a conversation allowance included, and a hard cap on any overage so an AEP spike
cannot produce a surprise invoice.

*Once published:* the price is on the site — no call required to see it.

### "We already pay for a voice vendor." (ICP-2)

Then the question is not whether to have one, it is whether yours can answer a plan
question. Run the Gap Report across one downline segment and look at what comes back. If
your existing vendor is answering benefit questions correctly, we are not the right spend.

### "Will my contracted agents actually use it?" (ICP-2)

The honest answer is that this is the most common way rollouts like this fail, and it is
not a technology problem. Agents who did not choose the tool kill it by not using it. So
the rollout includes agent-facing comms that address "is this replacing me" directly, and
we pilot one segment and measure adoption before widening. If adoption in the first
segment is poor, that is the signal to stop, not to push.

### "Is this replacing my agents?" (Level 1, and it is always asked)

No, and the economics say so: it covers the hours nobody is there and the questions that
currently interrupt you. What reaches your agents is a caller who already understands
their options, with the conversation attached. Fewer repeat questions, warmer handoffs,
and nobody working Sunday evenings.

### "What happens when it doesn't know something?"

It says it does not know and hands to your agent. That behaviour is designed, published,
and the thing we would most like you to test in the sandbox.

### "Can I see a customer using it?"

Not yet. We are pre-revenue and recruiting design partners — there are no customers and I
am not going to invent one. What I can do is run the sandbox on your own plan documents,
which is better evidence than someone else's testimonial anyway.

## The two objections with no good answer yet

Recorded honestly so nobody improvises:

1. **"Who else uses this?"** There is no answer until there is a design partner. Do not
   deflect with vague plurals.
2. **"What's the price?"** There is no answer until OF-1 closes. This is the single
   largest unblock in the funnel and it is a founder decision, not a copy problem.
