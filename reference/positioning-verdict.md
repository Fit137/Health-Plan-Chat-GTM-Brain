# Positioning verdict — the fork that was settled

*Load this when someone proposes changing what we lead on. The reasoning is here so the
decision does not get reopened. Cross-referenced in `ops/decisions.md`.*

## The fork

The Sprint 1 brief asked how Health Plan Chat could lead the category "by delivering
unique features that focus on positioning vector 1 and vector 2."

Read literally, vectors 1 and 2 are **the two vectors our competitors lead**: deployment
speed and integration breadth. That reading was rejected.

## Why the literal reading was rejected

Three reasons, in order of weight:

1. **It is not a unique-feature strategy.** Out-executing Synthflow on setup speed is an
   execution race on someone else's chosen ground. Whoever wins it wins a vector that,
   by the analysis, defends nothing — buyers acquired on speed leave on speed.
2. **It contradicts the feature analysis.** All six Q1 differentiators are L3 Medicare
   plan IP. Zero L1 commodity features reach Q1. Deployment speed and integration breadth
   are L1 concerns. A strategy built on them is a strategy built on the layer where we
   have no advantage and no prospect of one.
3. **We are at 5.4 and 4.6 on those vectors.** Leading a category from last place on the
   two vectors you have chosen to compete on is not a plan.

## The verdict

**Lead on the two vectors we actually lead: plan-grounded answer depth (9.3) and
accessible entry for a small agency (8.7).**

Vector 1 still appears in the strategy — as the **gate condition**, not the strategy. We
must get deployment speed to credible, because a category judged on time-to-first-agent
will not evaluate a product that takes three weeks to stand up. But "get fast enough to
be evaluated" is a different objective from "win on speed", and conflating them is what
the literal reading would have done.

## What this decision did not change

- It did not deprioritise onboarding speed. It reframed it from a differentiator to a
  qualifying threshold, which raises its urgency rather than lowering it.
- It did not say integrations do not matter. It said breadth is not where we win, and a
  focused set that covers the CRMs this segment actually uses is sufficient.
- It did not change the feature roadmap. Appointment booking and SOA capture are still
  the two urgent gaps, for the same reason: table stakes lose deals.

## The counter-argument, kept on purpose

The strongest case against this verdict: plan-document parsing is replicable, so a
well-funded competitor could reach comparable plan depth within a year, at which point we
would be competing on the vectors we are last on.

The answer is that the moat is not the parsing — it is the accumulated per-agency corpus,
the compliance posture around it, and the county-level reuse effect that makes each new
customer in a market cheaper to win than the last. That answer holds only if we accumulate
faster than a competitor can start. Which puts the deployment-speed gate back at the
centre, and is why it is recorded as the binding constraint rather than a to-do.

**Decided 2026-08-24. Revisit only if a competitor ships comparable plan grounding at
comparable price, or if design partner data shows onboarding cannot be got under two
weeks.**
