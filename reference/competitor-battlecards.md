# Competitor battlecards

*Synthesized, not extracted. Load before any sales conversation or comparison page. Each
card leads with the rival's real strength — a card that pretends the competitor is weak
gets an operator caught the first time a prospect knows better.*

`last_reviewed: 2026-08-25`

---

## careCycle

**Their strength.** Genuine Medicare depth. Member-aware call handling with plan and
benefit context, built for operations that run real volume. If an agency can afford them,
they are a serious product and we should say so.

**Their gap.** Approximately $10,000 a month for 5–10 agents, behind a demo and custom
quote. They do not serve, and are not trying to serve, the independent agency.

**How we win.** We do not beat careCycle in a bake-off. We reach the agency careCycle
never calls. The frame is not "better than careCycle" — it is "the first product with this
depth that an agency your size can actually buy."

**If the prospect is evaluating both.** They are probably bigger than our ICP-1. Qualify
honestly: if they have the budget and the volume, careCycle may be the right answer today.
Say that. It costs one deal and buys the credibility that wins the next five.

**Do not say.** That careCycle's product is weak, or that we match their enterprise
delivery. Both are false and easy to disprove.

**Open question (Q-1).** Whether their plan-context handling covers pre-sale benefit Q&A
or only post-enrolment servicing. Until settled, do not claim they cannot answer a benefit
question.

## Coverage Voice

**Their strength.** The most direct competitor. Medicare-native, HIPAA and SOC 2
certified, live under the customer's own brand and domain in 48 hours, captures Scope of
Appointment in-call, ships sub-accounts across multiple agencies white-labelled. On speed
and compliance posture they are ahead of us today.

**Their gap.** Plan-specific answer depth. They capture and qualify the caller; they do
not answer what the dental maximum is on that caller's plan.

**How we win.** The sandbox. Put a real plan question from their own book to both
products. That is the entire argument and it does not need a slide.

**If the prospect raises SOC 2.** They will. Answer straight: we do not hold it, it is on
the roadmap, here is our current posture in detail. Then move to what the posture actually
protects against. Never imply parity.

**If the prospect raises SOA or the 48-hour launch.** Both are real advantages. Concede
them and reframe to what happens after the handoff: their agent transfers a qualified
caller who still has not had their question answered.

**Do not say.** That multi-agency deployment is unique to us. It is theirs, shipped
(OF-2).

## MedicareCopilot

**Their strength.** Breadth that compounds. CRM, quoting, carrier-direct enrolment,
commissions, compliance and marketing in one workspace. Each win removes that agency from
the market for years, because switching means replacing everything at once.

**Their gap.** Agent-facing. It makes the agent better; it does not answer the phone at
nine on a Sunday.

**How we win.** We are not a replacement decision. We sit alongside. The pitch is additive:
"keep your platform, cover the hours and the questions it cannot."

**Watch for.** If the prospect is mid-migration onto MedicareCopilot, we are competing for
attention, not for budget. Wait for the migration to finish.

## Synthflow

**Their strength.** Fastest setup in the category — 9.4, a working agent the same
afternoon, before sales is involved. 200+ integrations, 30+ languages, a large genuine
review corpus, G2 badges for fastest implementation and best estimated ROI.

**Their gap.** No Medicare plan knowledge at all. It is a horizontal voice platform, and
an excellent one.

**How we win.** One question: "ask it what the dental allowance is on the plan you sold
last week." It is not a fair fight and it is not meant to be — the point is that the
prospect's requirement is Medicare-specific and they had not framed it that way yet.

**Where they beat us.** Speed and integrations, decisively. If the agency's real
requirement is a fast general receptionist, Synthflow is the better buy and we should not
fight for it.

**Do not say.** That Synthflow "does Medicare badly". It does not do Medicare. Misstating
this is an unforced credibility loss.

---

## SunFire

**Their strength.** Consumer-facing plan shopping and comparison, at scale, established.

**Their gap.** Not an answering product. Different job entirely.

**How we win.** Rarely a real competitor. If it comes up, the distinction is that SunFire
helps someone shop; we answer the person who already called the agency.

---

## Smith.ai

**Their strength.** Human-plus-AI answering with real reliability and years of trust. A
safe choice for an agency that wants a person in the loop.

**Their gap.** Generic. Their receptionist cannot answer a plan question any better than
the agency's own front desk can.

**How we win.** The Gap Report. Their own line, failing their own callers' questions,
recorded. Smith.ai does not change that recording; we do.

**Price context.** Smith.ai Basic normalises to roughly $600 per agency per month, which
is above our proposed Hero tier and buys less plan capability. Internal use only until
our own price is published.

---

## The one-line frame for all six

Every competitor either **captures the caller** or **informs the agent**. None answers the
beneficiary. That is the whole map, and it holds against all six.
