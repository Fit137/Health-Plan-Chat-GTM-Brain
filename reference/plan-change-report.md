# D3 · The 2027 Plan Change Report

*The top-of-funnel deliverable. Load before offering it, building one, or writing copy that
references it.*

`last_reviewed: 2026-09-11`

## The offer in one line

Tell us the plans you sell, and we will read the 2027 Summary of Benefits against the 2026
version for each one and send back every change, cited to the document and page.

Input: a list of plan names. Output: a plan-by-plan change report, plus what entered or left
their county, in time for the calls that start in October.

## Why this and not a cheat sheet

Two earlier forms were tried and both were too thin to earn a reply.

**An audit score** told an agency it answered six of twenty questions. A criticism from a
stranger, which the agency cannot act on and has no reason to thank us for.

**An answer sheet** listed twenty questions and their answers. Useful to a front desk,
clerical to an owner, and nothing the owner did not believe they could produce themselves.

Neither told the owner anything they did not already know. **The change report does.** No
agency owner knows, on 11 September, exactly what moved on every plan in their book for next
year, because the documents that say so are only now being published and reading them is
hours of work per plan.

## The problem it reveals

Carriers must send an Annual Notice of Change to every member by 30 September. So in the
first fortnight of October, before enrollment even opens, the agency's own members start
calling to ask what the letter means for them.

That is a wave of plan-specific questions, about the agency's existing book, arriving in the
two weeks when the owner can least afford to be on the phone. An owner reads that sentence and
recognises last October.

The second reveal is in the report itself. An owner who sees that three plans in their book
cut a benefit for 2027 knows immediately which members are now a competitor's opportunity, and
that they have weeks rather than months to reach them.

## Where AI is named, and how

The method is stated plainly because the method is the point:

> AI does the reading, and every change is checked against the source document before it goes
> in the report.

That is not a pitch and it must not become one. It is an accurate description of how a
document-comparison job gets done at this volume, and it does three things at once. It
explains how the work is free. It shows the reader what AI is actually good for in their
business, which is reading plan documents rather than answering phones. And it establishes,
before we have claimed anything, that our discipline is checking output against the carrier's
own document.

**Do not name the product in the report or in the message that offers it.** The follow-up
conversation is where the product belongs, and it lands better after the reader has seen what
this method produces.

## What the report contains

**Section one, the plans they sell.** Per plan: premium, primary care and specialist copays,
out-of-pocket maximum, dental, vision, hearing, the over-the-counter allowance, transport, and
any network or prior-authorisation change. Each change shows the 2026 value, the 2027 value,
and the page of the Summary of Benefits it came from.

**Section two, their county.** Which Medicare Advantage plans entered the county for 2027 and
which left. The first is where the switching conversation is, the second is where their
members have no choice but to move.

## Build rules

**Every line cites the document and the page.** This is the discipline that makes the report
safe to hand to someone personally liable for what their agency tells a member, and it is the
same rule the product runs on.

**Every figure carries its plan year.** A benefit number without a year is wrong by January.

**Where the documents do not settle something, say so.** A report that flags one item as
unresolved is worth more than one that infers, and it models what our system does when it
declines and escalates.

**State that it is a research aid.** The agency verifies before advising a member. We are
reading published documents, not giving advice to a beneficiary, and the line between those
two is one this reader respects.

## Why now, and the data timing

The 2027 documents publish through late September, and the member letters land by the 30th. So
the honest promise is not "here it is today", it is **"tell me your plans and you will have it
the week the documents land."**

That timing is the reason to reply now rather than in October. It is also the reason this
offer expires: from 15 October the owner is unreachable until 7 December, and a change report
delivered in November is a report about a season that has already been lost.

## Why it paves the way

The report is our capability performed once, by hand, on their plans, for free.

The follow-up is the next sentence rather than a new subject. This is every change on four
plans, read once, correct until the documents move again. The product is those same documents,
read continuously, answering a member's question on the phone at nine on a Sunday, in the
agency's name, with the same citation discipline.

Nothing in that argument needs a roadmap capability. Do not let the follow-up drift into
appointment booking or Scope of Appointment capture, which are the two the reader asks about
next and neither ships.

## The audit, retained as a variant

Putting twenty benefit questions to an agency's line and recording what happens remains the
most powerful version of D3 where it can be pre-run, because a recording of the prospect's own
line failing competes with nothing. Keep it for the highest-scoring rows only, run blind before
the first touch. The change report is the form that works cold, at scale, without a phone call.
