# Writing rules

Load with every writing task. Mechanics are checkable; check them.

`last_reviewed: 2026-08-25`

## 1. Mechanics

- Banned words: seamless, revolutionary, cutting-edge, game-changer, unlock, leverage, elevate, supercharge, robust, holistic, best-in-class, world-class, effortless, delve, tapestry, testament
- Banned phrases, which the linter cannot catch on its own: "in today's fast-paced",
  "landscape" as a metaphor, "at the end of the day". The banned list above covers the
  obvious variants of each word, so "leveraging" and "unlocked" fail too.
- **No em-dash as a stylistic tic.** One per page at most, where it does real work.
- **No exclamation marks.** Not one.
- **No rhetorical questions as headlines.** "Missing calls?" is not a headline.
- **Paragraphs under four lines.** Sentences under twenty-five words on average.
- **Specifics beat adjectives.** "The dental maximum on their plan" beats "comprehensive
  benefit information". If you cannot name the specific, you do not know it yet.
- **Numbers keep their units and their date.** "$694 per initial enrollment, 2026 CMS
  rate" — never a bare number.
- **No bold for emphasis in body copy.** Bold is for labels and terms.
- Oxford comma. Sentence case in headings. American spelling in customer-facing copy.

## 2. Tone

Direct, concrete, and slightly under-sold. The register of a competent operator talking
to another competent operator.

The reason this fits: the reader is a licensed insurance professional who carries personal
liability for what their front line says to a beneficiary, who has been sold to badly by
software vendors for a decade, and who is busiest exactly when we most want their
attention. Enthusiasm reads as inexperience to this reader. Precision reads as respect.

Under-claim rather than over-claim. This audience checks.

## 3. Writing for the ear

Applies to video, webinar, demo narration and any voice script.

- Short sentences. One idea each. No subordinate clauses stacked.
- Say the number, then what it means. "Ten thousand dollars a month. That is what the only
  comparable product costs."
- Never read a table aloud. Say the one number that matters.
- Compliance language is not cuttable for pacing: the TPMO disclaimer, the recording
  notice and the escalation behaviour get stated in full.
- Never narrate a roadmap capability as though it exists. See `feature-status.md`.

## 4. Claim discipline

Three triggers force a check against `do-not-say.md` and `feature-status.md` before the
sentence is allowed to stand:

1. **Any superlative.** "Only", "first", "best", "never", "always", "no other".
2. **Any capability mention.** Every one carries a status flag, and the flag decides the
   tense you may use.
3. **Any conditional claim.** If it is true in one configuration and false in another, the
   configuration is part of the claim.

A fourth, specific to this business: **any number about a customer outcome.** We have no
customers. Every outcome number is modelled, and must be labelled as modelled.

## 5. Channel discipline

**We write for:** personalised cold outreach to agency owners, agent communities and
forums, AEP prep webinars, LinkedIn outreach and ads targeted by job title, FMO and
carrier co-marketing, our own website and email nurture.

**We refuse to write for:** consumer-facing advertising of any kind, anything targeted at
Medicare beneficiaries, paid search on beneficiary terms, cold calling agencies, and
generic mass email. If asked for one of these, say the GTM excludes it and offer the
nearest channel that is in the mix. Do not produce a softened version.

**Segmentation is a hard rule.** An asset serves ICP-1 or ICP-2, never both. The signal
for which track a prospect is on is which value driver they completed: D1 or D2 implies
ICP-1, D5 or D6 implies ICP-2. An ICP-1 asset must not mention procurement or security
review. An ICP-2 asset must not mention month-to-month pricing.

## 6. Approved signature lines

Reusable verbatim. Everything here has passed the claim ledger.

- "The only AI that already knows the plans you sell."
- "Your callers ask about their plan, not about Medicare in general."
- "Nobody calls to ask what Medicare Advantage is."
- "It already knows your plans."
- "Every answer has a source."
- "You cannot hire your way to an eleven-o'clock Saturday."
- "The person who answered the question is the person they trust to write the
  application."
- "A missed call on a Sunday is not a missed call. It is a lost enrollment."
- "It does not guess. It escalates."

## 7. Formatting

- Tables where the content is comparative. Prose where it is an argument.
- Never a bulleted list of adjectives.
- One idea per bullet; if a bullet needs a semicolon it is two bullets.
- Every asset ends with one call to action, not three.
- Cite the plan year on any benefit example. Benefits change annually and an undated
  example is wrong by January.
