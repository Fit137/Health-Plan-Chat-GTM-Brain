# Glossary — canonical vocabulary

Load with every writing task. Names first: every other rule refers to things by name.

`last_reviewed: 2026-08-25`

## Canonical terms

| Term | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Health Plan Chat** | Three words, all capitalised, no hyphen. Never "HPC", "HealthPlanChat" or "Health Plan Chat AI" in external copy. |
| **Plan corpus** | The ingested Summary of Benefits and Evidence of Coverage documents for one agency's plans. Singular per agency. The asset that compounds. |
| **Benefit answer** | A response to a plan-specific coverage question. The unit of value. |
| **Conversation** | One complete caller interaction. The unit of billing. Not interchangeable with "benefit answer". |
| **Beneficiary** | The person on Medicare who calls. |
| **Agency** | Our customer. An independent Medicare insurance agency. |
| **Licensed agent** | A human, state-licensed, appointed with carriers. The escalation target. |
| **Downline** | Contracted agents or agencies selling under a GA or FMO. |
| **FMO** | Field Marketing Organisation. Spell out on first use. |
| **GA** | General Agency. Spell out on first use. |
| **AEP** | Annual Enrollment Period, 15 October – 7 December. Spell out on first use. |
| **OEP** | Open Enrollment Period, January – March. |
| **TPMO** | Third-Party Marketing Organisation. A CMS category that our customers fall into. |
| **SOA** | Scope of Appointment. Spell out on first use — it is a compliance term, not jargon. |
| **SB / EOC** | Summary of Benefits / Evidence of Coverage. The carrier documents we ingest. |
| **Plan-grounded** | Adjective for an answer that resolves against a carrier document. Our word. Use it. |

## Banned near-synonyms

| Do not use | Why | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Patient" | Medical framing. We are not in care delivery. | Beneficiary, or caller |
| "Customer" for the beneficiary | The agency is the customer. | Beneficiary, or caller |
| "Knowledge base" / "training data" | Generic, and it makes the corpus sound like a commodity. | Plan corpus |
| "Chatbot" / "bot" | Category we are trying to escape. Our own copy must not put us there. | AI front line, voice agent |
| "Receptionist" | The $79/month category. Using their word prices us into it. | Front line |
| "Lead" for a beneficiary mid-call | They are a person asking a question until they are qualified. | Caller |
| "Medicare.gov", "CMS-approved", "Medicare-endorsed" | Implies a government relationship we do not have. Regulatory exposure. | "Follows CMS marketing rules" |
| "Enrol the caller" | We do not enrol. A licensed agent does. | "Hands to your licensed agent" |
| "Client" for an agency | Consultancy register, wrong for a product. | Agency, or customer |

## Distinctions that must never blur

**Beneficiary-facing vs agent-facing.** We answer the *beneficiary*. MedicareCopilot and
SunFire inform the *agent*. careCycle and Coverage Voice capture the *lead*. Every
competitive claim we make turns on this distinction, and blurring it hands a prospect a
reason to think we are the same product as something cheaper.

**Plan-grounded vs Medicare-general.** An answer about *what Medicare Advantage is* is
Medicare-general and any model can produce it. An answer about *the dental maximum on
H1234-005 in Pinellas County for plan year 2026* is plan-grounded and is the only thing
we win on. Never let copy imply the first is the second.

**Conversation vs benefit answer.** One conversation may contain several benefit answers,
or none. Pricing counts conversations. Value is measured in benefit answers. Mixing the
two in the same sentence produces a claim that cannot be substantiated.

---

**The one that matters most.** *Plan-grounded* is a claim about provenance, not about
quality. It means the answer resolves against a specific carrier document that we can
produce on request. If we cannot produce the document, the answer was not plan-grounded
and the word must not be used.
